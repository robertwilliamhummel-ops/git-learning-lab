## The $30,000 Small Supplier Rule

### 📌 The Basic Rule

**If your business makes UNDER $30,000 in revenue:**
- ✅ You're a "Small Supplier"
- ✅ You DON'T need to register for HST
- ✅ You DON'T charge HST on invoices
- ✅ You DON'T collect or remit HST to CRA
- ✅ This is 100% LEGAL

**If your business makes $30,000 OR MORE:**
- ❌ You're NO LONGER a Small Supplier
- ✅ You MUST register for HST within 29 days
- ✅ You MUST charge HST (13% in Ontario) on all future invoices
- ✅ You MUST file HST returns (quarterly or annually)
- ✅ This is REQUIRED BY LAW

### 📊 How the $30K is Calculated

**Method 1: Rolling 4 Quarters**
```
Add up revenue from last 4 consecutive calendar quarters

Example:
Q1 2025: $8,000
Q2 2025: $7,500
Q3 2025: $9,000
Q4 2025: $6,000
Total: $30,500 → MUST REGISTER!
```

**Method 2: Single Calendar Quarter**
```
If you make $30K+ in any single quarter

Example:
Q3 2025: $32,000 in one quarter
→ MUST REGISTER immediately!
```

**What Counts as Revenue:**
- All taxable sales and services
- Before expenses
- The amount BEFORE HST (if you're already registered)

### ⚠️ CRITICAL: You CANNOT Stay Under $30K on Purpose

**ILLEGAL (Tax Evasion):**
- ❌ Refusing work to stay under $30K
- ❌ Not reporting income to stay under $30K
- ❌ Hiding revenue to avoid registration
- ❌ Splitting income across multiple fake businesses

**LEGAL:**
- ✅ Starting a new business under $30K
- ✅ Having a slow year that stays under $30K naturally
- ✅ Part-time business that doesn't hit $30K

**Penalties for Evasion:**
- 50% penalty on unpaid HST + interest
- CRA audits and investigations
- Possible criminal charges
- Business reputation destroyed
- **NOT WORTH IT!**

## How HST Works (The Basics)

### 🎯 HST is a "Pass-Through" Tax

**Key Concept: HST is NOT YOUR MONEY**

You're just a tax collector for the government:

```
Customer pays: $1,130 ($1,000 service + $130 HST)

You keep: $1,000 (your revenue)
You hold: $130 (government's money)
You remit: $130 to CRA on HST return

Net to you: $1,000 (same as if no HST!)
```

### 📝 HST Rates in Canada

**Ontario (HST): 13%**
- Federal GST: 5%
- Provincial PST: 8%
- Combined HST: 13%

**Other Provinces:**
- BC: 12% (GST + PST)
- Alberta: 5% (GST only)
- Quebec: 14.975% (GST + QST)
- Atlantic provinces: 15% (HST)

*This guide focuses on Ontario's 13% HST*

### 🔄 The HST Cycle

**Step 1: You Collect HST from Customers**
```
Invoice to customer:
Service: $1,000
HST (13%): $130
Total: $1,130

You receive $1,130
```

**Step 2: You Pay HST on Business Purchases**
```
Buy laptop for business:
Laptop: $1,000
HST (13%): $130
Total: $1,130

You pay $1,130
```

**Step 3: You File HST Return**
```
HST collected: $130
HST paid: $130
Net owing: $0

You owe nothing! (They cancel out)
```

## HST vs Business Expenses (CRITICAL!)

### ⚠️ MOST IMPORTANT SECTION - READ CAREFULLY!

**These are TWO COMPLETELY SEPARATE THINGS:**

### 1️⃣ BUSINESS EXPENSE (Income Tax Deduction)

**What It Is:**
- Reduces your TAXABLE INCOME
- Filed on your T1 or T2 tax return
- Saves you INCOME TAX (not HST)

**How It Works:**

**Scenario A: You DON'T charge HST (under $30K)**
```
Your Invoice to Client:
- Service: $1,000
- HST: $0
- Total: $1,000

Client's Income Tax Return:
- Claims $1,000 as business expense
- Reduces taxable income by $1,000
- Saves ~$250-400 in income tax (depending on rate)

They ALWAYS claim the expense!
```

**Scenario B: You DO charge HST (over $30K)**
```
Your Invoice to Client:
- Service: $1,000
- HST: $130
- Total: $1,130

Client's Income Tax Return:
- Claims $1,000 as business expense (NOT $1,130!)
- Reduces taxable income by $1,000
- Saves ~$250-400 in income tax

SAME TAX SAVINGS!
```

**KEY POINT:**
```
The business expense deduction is ALWAYS just the service cost
HST is NOT included in the expense
Whether you charge HST or not, their expense deduction is THE SAME
```

### 2️⃣ INPUT TAX CREDIT (HST Refund)

**What It Is:**
- Gets back HST paid on business purchases
- Filed on your HST return (separate from income tax)
- Gets back the ACTUAL HST DOLLARS

**How It Works:**

**Only Applies if You CHARGE HST:**

```
Your Invoice (with HST):
- Service: $1,000
- HST: $130
- Total: $1,130

Client's HST Return (separate from income tax!):
- "I paid $130 HST to TechFlow"
- CRA refunds them $130
- Net HST cost: $0

This is SEPARATE from the expense deduction!
```

**If You DON'T Charge HST:**

```
Your Invoice (no HST):
- Service: $1,000
- Total: $1,000

Client's HST Return:
- Nothing to claim (you didn't charge HST)
- No HST refund
- But they also didn't pay any HST!

Net result: SAME as above!
```

### 🔍 The Complete Picture

**Business Client Pays You $1,000 for Service:**

**Option A: You're Under $30K (No HST)**
```
Invoice: $1,000
They pay: $1,000

Income Tax: Claim $1,000 expense, save $300
HST Return: Nothing to claim
NET COST: $700
```

**Option B: You're Over $30K (With HST)**
```
Invoice: $1,000 + $130 HST = $1,130
They pay: $1,130

Income Tax: Claim $1,000 expense, save $300
HST Return: Claim $130 ITC, get $130 back
NET COST: $700

SAME RESULT!
```

**This is why business customers don't care if you charge HST!**

## Input Tax Credits (Getting Money Back)

### 💰 How YOU Get HST Back on Business Purchases

**The Rule:**
```
If you're REGISTERED for HST:
→ You can claim back HST on ALL business purchases
→ This is called "Input Tax Credit" (ITC)
→ You get the actual HST dollars back
```

### ✅ What You CAN Claim HST Back On

**Services:**
- ✅ Accountant: $500 + $65 HST → Claim $65 back
- ✅ Lawyer: $1,000 + $130 HST → Claim $130 back
- ✅ Web hosting: $100 + $13 HST → Claim $13 back
- ✅ Office rent: $1,500 + $195 HST → Claim $195 back
- ✅ Consultant fees: Any business service with HST

**Equipment & Supplies:**
- ✅ Laptop (business): $1,000 + $130 HST → Claim $130 back
- ✅ Office furniture: $500 + $65 HST → Claim $65 back
- ✅ Printer: $300 + $39 HST → Claim $39 back
- ✅ Office supplies: $100 + $13 HST → Claim $13 back
- ✅ Software subscriptions: $50 + $6.50 HST → Claim $6.50 back

**Vehicle (if business use):**
- ✅ Gas for business trips → Claim HST back
- ✅ Vehicle purchase (business %) → Claim HST back
- ✅ Repairs & maintenance → Claim HST back
- ✅ Insurance (business %) → Claim HST back

**Utilities (if home office):**
- ✅ Internet (business %) → Claim HST back
- ✅ Phone (business %) → Claim HST back
- ✅ Electricity (business %) → Claim HST back

### ❌ What You CANNOT Claim HST Back On

**Personal Purchases:**
- ❌ Personal groceries → NO HST back
- ❌ Personal clothing → NO HST back
- ❌ Personal meals → NO HST back
- ❌ Personal entertainment → NO HST back
- ❌ Personal Netflix → NO HST back

**Mixed Use (Requires Allocation):**
- ⚠️ Vehicle (50% business) → Claim 50% of HST
- ⚠️ Home office (20% of home) → Claim 20% of HST
- ⚠️ Phone (70% business) → Claim 70% of HST

**The Golden Rule:**
```
BUSINESS purchase → Claim HST back
PERSONAL purchase → Cannot claim HST
```

### 📊 Real-World Example: Your HST Return

**Month 1 of Being HST Registered:**

**You Collect HST (from customers):**
```
Client A: $2,000 + $260 HST
Client B: $1,500 + $195 HST
Client C: $1,000 + $130 HST
Client D: $500 + $65 HST

Total HST Collected: $650
```

**You Pay HST (on business purchases):**
```
New laptop: $1,000 + $130 HST
Web hosting: $100 + $13 HST
Office supplies: $200 + $26 HST
Accountant: $500 + $65 HST
Gas (business): $100 + $13 HST

Total HST Paid: $247
```

**Your HST Return:**
```
HST Collected: $650
HST Paid (ITCs): -$247
Net HST Owing: $403

You send $403 to CRA
```

**But you saved $247 on your business purchases!**

### 💸 When CRA Refunds YOU

**Scenario: You paid more HST than you collected**

```
Month 2:
HST Collected: $200
HST Paid: $500
Difference: -$300

CRA REFUNDS you $300!
```

**This happens when:**
- Slow sales month
- Big equipment purchases
- Starting out with lots of setup costs
- More expenses than revenue

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### ❌ MISTAKE #1: Trying to Stay Under $30K Forever

**Wrong Thinking:**
"I'll refuse work to stay under $30K and avoid HST"

**Why It's Wrong:**
- Caps your income at $30K/year
- Illegal if you hide revenue
- Limits business growth
- Miss out on claiming HST back on purchases

**Right Approach:**
- Grow naturally
- Register when you cross $30K
- Charge HST and claim it back on expenses
- Scale to $50K, $100K, $200K+

### ❌ MISTAKE #2: Thinking HST "Costs" You Money

**Wrong Thinking:**
"If I charge HST, I lose 13% of my income"

**Why It's Wrong:**
- HST is NOT your money
- Customer pays the HST
- You just collect and remit it
- Your revenue stays the same

**Example:**
```
Service: $1,000
HST: $130
Total: $1,130

Customer pays: $1,130
You keep: $1,000 (your revenue)
You send CRA: $130 (their money)

You didn't lose anything!
```

### ❌ MISTAKE #3: Not Claiming Input Tax Credits

**Wrong Thinking:**
"Filing HST returns is too much hassle"

**Why It's Wrong:**
- You're leaving money on the table
- HST on business purchases adds up fast
- Easy to claim with proper receipts

**Example:**
```
Annual business expenses: $20,000
HST on those expenses: $2,600

If you don't claim: You lose $2,600!
If you do claim: CRA refunds you $2,600!
```

### ❌ MISTAKE #4: Confusing Expense Deduction with HST

**Wrong Thinking:**
"If I don't charge HST, clients can't claim my service as an expense"

**Why It's Wrong:**
- Expense deduction and HST are SEPARATE
- Clients ALWAYS claim the service cost as an expense
- HST is a separate, additional credit
- Both work independently

**Reality:**
```
With or without HST:
- Client claims service cost as expense (income tax)
- Client saves ~25-40% on income tax
- HST (if charged) is claimed separately
- Net cost is the same either way
```

### ❌ MISTAKE #5: Claiming HST on Personal Purchases

**Wrong Thinking:**
"I'll just claim HST on everything I buy"

**Why It's Wrong:**
- CRA audits and catches this
- Penalties are severe
- Only business purchases qualify
- Personal use is not deductible

**What Happens:**
```
Buy personal laptop ($1,000 + $130 HST)
Claim $130 ITC incorrectly

CRA Audit:
- Disallow the $130 credit
- Charge interest
- Possible penalties
- Repeat offenders face prosecution
```
